Reading Reflections

"In Plato's Cave" by Susan Sontag

Taking a picture is something that has become second nature to me. Ever since I was a child I took any camera I could find and took pictures of whatever I desired, I would use disposable film cameras, digital cameras, or my mom’s flip phone camera almost anything I could find. I had memories as a kid using my older brother’s digital camera to take pictures while on a trip to the Dominican Republic. I loved using it to take pictures of anything I thought was worth capturing. My brother eventually let me keep it and I would take it to school and take pictures of my friends and I. This love of photography didn’t end there, as I got older I still had a desire to take pictures. Although I didn’t explore more of this until I was in my freshman year of college, when I took a photography class. This is where I was able to learn more about the basics and my excitement for it grew. Now when I take pictures I still use what I learned constantly. I capture images mainly with my iphone camera or sometimes a disposable camera. It usually consists of things I come across that motivate me to pull out my camera, from a pretty sunset, something sentimental, or an image so intriguing I can’t pass up the opportunity to not take a picture. A lot of the images could be meaningless, funny, or beautiful to me. However capturing these images I always find to be satisfying and an activity I will always adore.

Capturing images can be resourceful and powerful. As stated in Susan Sontag’s, In Plato’s Cave “Photographs furnish evidence… A photograph passes for incontrovertible proof that a given thing happened” and “photography is not practiced by most people as an art. It is mainly a social rite, a defense against anxiety, and a tool of power.” Sontag perfectly captured how photography is used and how it is used to represent our world currently. Using photography to capture these aspects of our world can be very powerful, especially with the political climate of the world currently. The use of ads, photos as evidence, and propaganda are just some examples of how photography and illustration is used to represent the world at the moment. It is constantly used in our daily lives and becomes a part of it. Capturing a moment or using illustration to create something to send a message has become a major part of what we see constantly on social media. Some posts can be meaningless or be intended to send a message; such as a candidate's political campaigns, a person’s selfie, or a news article's instagram post. All of this uses a combination of image and text to convey their point to the viewer and is able to reach a wide range of people. It is the most effective way to grab people’s attention now since the start of photography being used in news articles. As Sontag stated “Photographs are valued because they give information. They tell one what there is; they make an inventory.”
